The narrator of a novel is not the author.
Every story is told by a voice that the writer invents for the occasion, and the distance between that voice and the truth is the essential subject of modern fiction.
The main character of the book believes his own account of the events.
The reader, who sees more than the narrator does, discovers the irony gradually.
A great novel therefore teaches a double reading: we follow the plot, and at the same time we measure the narrator against it.
The metaphor of the mirror appears in every chapter, and the image of the broken glass returns at the end of the tragedy.
Critics have argued about the meaning of the final scene for a century.
The style of the prose, with its long sentences and its careful rhythm, creates the emotion that the plot alone could not produce.
