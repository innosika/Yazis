Neural machine translation has become the dominant approach in computational linguistics.
The model learns distributed representations of words from large parallel corpora.
An encoder maps the source sentence into a sequence of hidden states, and a decoder generates the target sentence one token at a time.
The attention mechanism computes a weighted sum of the encoder states for every decoding step.
Researchers evaluate the quality of a system with automatic metrics such as BLEU.
The training procedure minimises the cross-entropy loss with stochastic gradient descent.
Modern systems process a large amount of data on graphics hardware, and the network contains billions of parameters.
However, the output of a statistical system remains difficult to interpret, because the internal representation carries no explicit grammatical information.
A rule-based system, by contrast, describes the transfer between two languages with an explicit dictionary and a set of syntactic rules.
